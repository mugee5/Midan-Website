Who We Work With

Three kinds of partners.
One shared purpose.

Early Users
Schools, Listed Companies & Event Facilitators

The institutions and individuals facing the problems we're building into. We want early users who'll tell us honestly when the product isn't working — and be the first proof that it does. In exchange: early access, direct input into the product roadmap, and pricing that reflects the relationship rather than the market rate.

Domain Partners
NGOs, Researchers & Ground Truth Holders

Organisations with deep expertise in education, healthcare, or the green economy who bring institutional reach and field knowledge we can't substitute. The terms are always the same: open data, defined deliverables, and Midān retains full analytical independence. We bring the technical infrastructure; you bring the context that makes it matter.

Team
Builders Who Want the Long Game

We're not hiring in any formal sense yet. But we're always interested in people who want to build with a 20-year horizon — developers, designers, researchers, and people who understand the Indian institutional context from the inside. If that's you, write and tell us what you'd want to build here.

What a Collaboration Looks Like

What Midān
actually brings.

We're not a full-stack agency that does whatever the brief asks. We have a specific set of capabilities and a specific way of working — built around producing infrastructure that compounds, not deliverables that sit in a folder.

The right partners are the ones who want what we actually have, not a version of it reshaped to fit an existing procurement frame. We work in sectors we understand, on problems we've already validated, with partners who share our commitment to honest outcomes.

Working software — not wireframes

Not decks with product screenshots. Not prototypes that need another phase. Working software, built lean, that the people it's meant to serve can actually use from day one. We ship things.

Data infrastructure and systems thinking

We build the operational backbone — data models, automation layers, reporting pipelines — not just the interface. The kind of infrastructure that makes institutions run more honestly, and shows where they aren't.

Editorial and analytical independence

We don't adjust our findings to match what a partner wants to hear. If the data says an intervention isn't working, we say so. That's not a risk in a collaboration — it's the whole point of bringing us in.

A long-term perspective

We're not optimising for the deliverable. We're optimising for the outcome — what the person the system is built for actually experiences two years from now. Every engagement is evaluated against that standard.

How We Operate

The foundations
that don't move.

We don't manufacture dissatisfaction to sell software. We don't compromise our data or our findings for a client or a funder. We don't build things that extract value from the people they're meant to serve. These aren't constraints on our ambition — they're what makes it worth having.

I — Value
Genuine value over manufactured attention

Every product creates real benefit or it doesn't ship. We don't exploit time or attention to make numbers look good. Trust, earned slowly, compounds in ways that growth hacks don't.

II — Integrity
Sufficiency as a strategic position

We don't need to manufacture dissatisfaction to sell our products — because they work. Operating from a place of principle, not urgency, is what allows us to say no to the wrong partnerships and hold the line on our findings. The long-term credibility that comes from that is worth more than whatever we'd gain by abandoning it.

III — Independence
We work with institutions. We are not owned by them.

Full editorial and analytical independence — always. A company that depends on a single funder cannot hold that funder accountable. We're built to avoid that dependency by design.

IV — Horizon
Every decision made with the 20-year view

Not the next funding cycle. Not the quarterly number. The body of work someone can look back on with honest pride, and the institutional accountability it helped create.

The Boundaries
What Midān
won't do.

These aren't negotiating positions. They're the lines that keep the mission coherent. A company that crosses them once has a different relationship with integrity than one that never did.

— Sign NDAs on civic or public interest work. If it affects citizens, citizens can see the data. Full stop.
— Adjust our findings for a client or funder. We share what the data shows. Partners who need different findings need a different partner.
— Become government-dependent. We work with government as a partner on defined projects — never as the primary revenue source that shapes our positions.
— Take retainer work that doesn't compound. We build products. Custom work for single clients is a distraction unless it validates a product direction.
— Build products that exploit the people they serve. No dark patterns. No manufactured dissatisfaction. No extracting attention to sell something else.
